Jalon Privacy Policy (Beta)

Jalon · Arctura Technologies Inc. · Version beta-1 · Effective November 11, 2026
This is the privacy policy for the beta version of Jalon. Jalon is still being built and tested, and some features described here are available only on some platforms or to some testers. We will replace this policy before Jalon’s public release.

This Privacy Policy explains how Arctura Technologies Inc. (“Arctura,” “we,” “us” or “our”) handles personal information in Jalon (佳朗), our preparation service for the TCF, TEF, DELF and DALF French exams. It covers the Jalon apps for iPhone, iPad and Android, Jalon on the web and the services behind them (together, “Jalon”). Arctura is responsible for your personal information in Jalon.

In short

  • You can use Jalon without signing up. We create an anonymous account for you; if you don’t sign in within 30 days, it’s deleted with its data.
  • We don’t sell your information, show ads or track you across other companies’ apps. Jalon has no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting tools.
  • When you practise speaking, your voice is processed by OpenAI in the United States, and your recordings are deleted after 90 days.
  • Your recordings and essays are used to improve our scoring only if you turn that on. It’s off by default.
  • Recalls you submit become part of the question bank without your name or account.
  • For any privacy question or request, email privacy@arcturatech.com.

1. Information we collect

Account and sign-in

  • Anonymous account. The first time you open Jalon, we create an account with a random account number, so your practice can be saved without signing up.
  • Email sign-in. If you sign in with your email address, we use it to send you a 6-digit code.
  • Sign in with Apple or Google. We receive an identifier for your Apple or Google account and the email address it shares with us. Apple can share a private relay address instead of your real one. Google also shares the name and profile photo link on your Google account; we keep them with your sign-in details but don’t use them in Jalon. We never receive your Apple or Google password.
  • Device ID. Each app installation or browser creates a random device ID, which is sent with your practice records so they sync correctly between your devices. It isn’t your device’s advertising identifier or serial number.

Your study profile

The exam you’re preparing for, your exam date (or that you haven’t registered yet), why you’re taking it (for example, Canadian immigration or citizenship, living in France, or study), your daily study time, your target scores, the exams you’d like to hear about, your interface language, your time zone and whether you want to be told about new questions.

Practice and the question bank

  • Practice records. Your answers, whether they were correct, how long you took and the mode (practice, mock exam, placement test or quick practice).
  • “I got this one” marks. When you mark a question you met in your exam: the question, your exam date and the test city you choose.
  • Recalls. When you submit what you remember from your exam: what you write, the exam, the date, the test city, the section and the task (see section 5).
  • Question reports. The reason and the note you give.
  • Search. What you search for is used to find results and isn’t saved to your account.

Writing

Your essays as you write them, their word counts, when and from which device each version was saved, earlier versions kept as history (the 20 most recent for each task) and when you submitted.

Speaking with the AI examiner

  • Recordings. During a speaking session, Jalon records your microphone (not the examiner’s voice) and uploads the recording in 10-second pieces.
  • Transcripts. A live transcript of what you and the examiner said, and a detailed transcript of your recording, with the timing of each word, which we use for scoring.
  • Session details. The exam and task, the mode, the examiner settings, start and end times, connection interruptions and why the session ended.

Choices and records

  • Consent choices. Whether you allowed training use of your recordings and essays (section 6) and, if you joined one, a research pilot, with the version of the text you saw and the time of each decision.
  • Free access and limits. Free access you earn, for example for recalls we accept, and how much of the daily free limits you’ve used.
  • AI usage records. For each AI request: the time, the model, the amount of processing and its estimated cost. These records don’t include what you said or wrote.

Technical information

When the app connects to our servers, our hosting provider receives your IP address, your device or browser type and the time of the request, and keeps them in its logs for a short period. Our sign-in service also records the IP address and device type of each sign-in session. We use this information to run Jalon and keep it secure. Our own logs leave out what you write and say. On the web, Jalon keeps your sign-in session in your browser’s storage and uses one cookie to remember your language. When you visit our website, its host receives similar technical information.

Messages and complaints

If you email us or send a copyright complaint, we collect your name, your email address and what you tell us.

What we don’t collect

We don’t collect your device’s location, contacts or photos, or your advertising identifier. Jalon doesn’t use advertising, analytics or crash-reporting tools. Some information stays on your device only, such as your settings and drafts that haven’t been saved to our servers yet.

2. How we use information

We use information to:

  • provide Jalon: save and sync your practice, run practice sessions, mock exams, the AI examiner and writing practice, and show your history, scores and progress;
  • keep the question bank up to date: turn recalls into questions and show how often and how recently each question has been seen;
  • apply free limits and give you the free access you earn;
  • send sign-in codes and reply to you;
  • keep Jalon secure, prevent abuse and fix problems;
  • improve our scoring models with your recordings and essays, only if you agree (section 6); and
  • comply with the law and enforce our Terms of Use.

We don’t sell your personal information, use it for advertising or track you across other companies’ apps and websites. Scores and feedback are estimates to help you practise. We don’t make decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects on you by automated means alone.

3. Legal bases

If you’re in the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom or Switzerland, we rely on:

  • contract, to provide Jalon to you: your account, sign-in, practice, sync, the AI examiner, writing, recalls and free access;
  • legitimate interests, to keep Jalon secure and prevent abuse, to build the question bank from recalls and “I got this one” marks without identifying you, to handle complaints and to keep a record of deleted accounts. You can object to this processing;
  • consent, for training use (section 6) and research pilots. You can withdraw it at any time; and
  • legal obligations, where the law requires us to keep or disclose information.

In Canada, including Québec, we rely on your consent. When you use a Jalon feature after reading this policy, you consent to the processing that feature needs. Training use and research pilots need your express consent, which stays off until you give it. You can withdraw your consent, subject to legal or contractual limits; some features can’t work without the information they need.

4. The AI examiner

  • Live conversation. Jalon connects your device directly to OpenAI’s Realtime service. Your voice is streamed to OpenAI, which produces the examiner’s spoken replies and the live transcript. OpenAI receives your device’s IP address.
  • Scoring transcript. After the session, our servers send your recording to OpenAI to produce the detailed transcript.
  • What’s sent. Your voice and the instructions for the exam task. We don’t send your name, email address or account number, but anything you say is part of the audio.
  • OpenAI’s role. OpenAI processes this data on our behalf. Under our agreement, it doesn’t use it to train its models, and it may keep it for up to 30 days to monitor for abuse.
  • Where. OpenAI processes this data in the United States. During the beta, this applies to all users, including users in the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. We’ve asked OpenAI to process data in Europe; once that’s available and our European data region is in use, the speaking data of users there will be processed in Europe, and we’ll update this policy first.
  • Accuracy. The examiner and the transcripts are produced by AI and can be wrong. Scores are estimates. Official results prevail.

5. Recalls and the question bank

Jalon’s past-paper bank is built partly from recalls that candidates submit after their exam.

  • Before a recall leaves your account’s data region, we automatically remove email addresses, phone numbers and long strings of digits from it.
  • The copy our editors work with doesn’t include your account number. Instead, it carries a code made from your account and exam date, used only to count how many different people reported a question. “I got this one” marks are counted the same way.
  • Editors may combine your recall with others, edit and translate it, and publish it in Jalon, including its original wording (Terms of Use, section 6).
  • Published questions stay in the bank if you later delete your recall or your account. Other users never see who contributed.
  • Please don’t include personal information about yourself or anyone else in a recall or a note.

6. Training consent and research pilots

  • Off by default. The setting “Allow my recordings and essays to improve the scoring model” is off unless you turn it on.
  • If you turn it on, the recordings and essays you make while it’s on may be de-identified, annotated by certified examiners and used to train and test the models that estimate speaking and writing scores.
  • You can turn it off at any time in the app or by emailing us. From then on, none of your recordings or essays go into new training batches, including those made while it was on. Models already trained aren’t changed.
  • During the beta, this setting may not appear in your version of the app yet. Until you turn it on, nothing of yours is used for training.
  • Research pilots. We may invite some testers to a research pilot that compares our AI scoring with certified examiners. Joining takes a separate consent that explains what’s collected and how it’s used. Samples are de-identified before examiners see them, and you can withdraw at any time.

7. How we share information

We share personal information only:

  • with service providers that process it on our behalf, under contracts that require them to protect it and to use it only on our instructions:
    • Supabase hosts our database, file storage and server functions, and sends sign-in code emails;
    • OpenAI runs the AI examiner and produces transcripts (section 4);
  • with Apple and Google, which provide Sign in with Apple, Sign in with Google and app distribution under their own terms. If you test Jalon through TestFlight or Google Play testing, Apple or Google may also share with us the name and email address you used to join, and the crash reports and feedback you choose to send;
  • with other users, only as published questions built from recalls, never with your name or account;
  • if required by law, or if needed to protect the rights, property or safety of our users, us or others; and
  • as part of a merger, acquisition, financing or sale of assets, subject to this policy.

8. Where your information is stored

  • Arctura is based in Ontario, Canada.
  • During the beta, every account is stored in Canada, in Supabase’s data centre in Montréal, Québec.
  • We’ve also set up a data region in Paris, France, for users in the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. It isn’t used for accounts yet. When it is, the practice records, recordings, transcripts and essays of new accounts from those countries will be stored there, while sign-in details (email address and Apple or Google identifier) will stay in Canada. We’ll update this policy first.
  • OpenAI processes speaking data in the United States (section 4). Supabase is a US company, and its staff may access data from other countries to provide support.
  • Your information may therefore be processed outside the country where you live, where authorities may be able to access it under local law.
  • For transfers out of the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, we rely on adequacy decisions where they apply, such as the European Commission’s decision on Canada, and otherwise on standard contractual clauses in our agreements with our providers. Where Québec law requires, we assess the protection of personal information before it’s communicated outside Québec.

9. Retention and deletion

We keep information as long as your account exists, unless a shorter period is given below, and longer only where the law requires it or to resolve disputes.

  • Anonymous accounts. If you don’t sign in within 30 days of first opening Jalon, your anonymous account is deleted with its data. If you delete the app before signing in, you can’t get that data back.
  • Recordings. Deleted automatically 90 days after the session ends. You can delete them sooner in the app where it offers this, or by emailing us.
  • Transcripts. When a recording is deleted after 90 days, its detailed transcript is kept so that your session results keep working. If you delete a recording yourself, its detailed transcript is deleted with it. The live transcript stays with your session history. Both are deleted with your account.
  • Essays, practice records and your profile. Kept while your account exists. Writing history keeps the 20 most recent earlier versions of each task.
  • Published questions and the coded copies of recalls and “I got this one” marks are kept as part of the question bank (section 5).
  • Copyright complaints. Your name and contact details are deleted 90 days after your complaint is closed.
  • Logs. Our hosting provider keeps request logs for a short period. OpenAI may keep data for up to 30 days (section 4).
  • Signing in to an account you already have. If you used Jalon without signing in and then sign in to an existing account, your practice records, recalls, marks, recordings, transcripts and essays move to that account. Where both accounts have a setting or a consent choice, the existing account’s is kept. The anonymous account is then deleted.
  • Deleting your account. During the beta, email privacy@arcturatech.com to delete your account; deletion in the app is coming. We’ll check that the request comes from you, for example by sending a code to the account’s email address, and then deactivate your account. You can change your mind within 30 days. After that, we permanently delete your account and its data, including recordings, transcripts, essays, practice records and your profile. We keep only the account number and the date it was deleted, without your email address or content, to handle refunds and audits. Copies in backups are deleted as the backups expire.

10. Your choices and rights

  • In Jalon, you can stay anonymous or sign in, turn training consent on or off and delete recordings, where your version of the app offers these. You can also turn off microphone access in your device settings at any time; speaking practice then won’t work.
  • Depending on where you live, you have the right to access your personal information, to get a copy in a portable format, to correct or delete it, to object to or restrict its processing, and to withdraw your consent.
  • During the beta, data export and account deletion are handled by email: write to privacy@arcturatech.com. We may need to verify your identity. We reply within 30 days, and we won’t treat you differently for exercising your rights.
  • You can complain to a data protection authority: in Canada, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada; in Québec, the Commission d’accès à l’information; in the EEA, the authority where you live or work; in the United Kingdom, the Information Commissioner’s Office. We’d appreciate the chance to address your concern first.
  • If you live in California, we don’t sell or share personal information, as those terms are defined under California law.

11. Security

We use reasonable technical and organizational measures to protect personal information. Data is encrypted in transit, and our hosting provider encrypts it at rest. Recordings are kept in private storage that the app can write to only through short-lived upload links. Only staff who need it can access personal information, and editors see recalls and question reports without your account. Our logs leave out what you write and say. No system is completely secure, so we can’t guarantee absolute security. If a security incident puts you at risk, we’ll notify you and the authorities as the law requires.

12. Children

Jalon isn’t for children. You must be at least 13 to use it, or 14 in Québec. In the EEA and the United Kingdom, you must also be at least the age at which you can consent to online services yourself in your country (between 13 and 16). During the beta, we can’t collect a parent’s consent, so if you’re under that age, please don’t use Jalon. If you believe a child has given us personal information, email privacy@arcturatech.com and we’ll delete it.

13. Beta and changes to this policy

This policy covers the beta and will be replaced before Jalon’s public release. When we change it, we’ll post the new version here with a new version number and effective date, and keep earlier versions available. For material changes, we’ll tell you in the app or by email before they take effect.
