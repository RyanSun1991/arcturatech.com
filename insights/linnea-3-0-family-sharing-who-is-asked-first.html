Linnea 3.0 is on the App Store. Here is who is asked before anything about your family leaves your account.

Linnea 3.0 reached the App Store on 9 November, and version 3.3 of Linnea's privacy policy took effect the same day; on Google Play, the Linnea page says 3.0 is rolling out in stages. The page lists what is new: starting without an account, seeing how each dish is counted, meal links for family, and one family plan for everyone at home. By our count, four things the policy now describes move something between people: family links, Family AI, the family plan and invitations. For a family health app, the question that matters most is not what is encrypted but who is asked before something about a person leaves the account. In 3.0 the answer differs for each of the four, and the policy says which.

Published 11 November 2026 · 5 min read · By Arctura Technologies

A meal, sent as a link

From a meal you logged, you can send a link to someone who is not on Linnea. Whoever opens it sees the name you chose for yourself, the meal, its date and the dish names, but no nutrition numbers. If they save it, their own portion, with its numbers, goes into their own log, with or without an account. A link works once, for up to seven days, and you can withdraw it until it is claimed; you see whether it was claimed, not who claimed it. The page the link opens uses no cookies or analytics; to stop abuse we keep, for up to two days, a code made from the visitor's IP address that we cannot turn back into the address.

Who is asked: you, when you send, and the person who opens the page, when they save. Nobody else. Two limits sit beside that: the policy asks you to share information about other people only if you are allowed to, and warns that what someone has already seen or saved cannot be recalled. Family links are not meant for children, it adds.

AI at the family table

With Premium, the Family Menu can use AI to plan for everyone at home; with a family plan, the weekly family summary can include a short AI-written paragraph. Family AI asks before it sends anything, and each member with an account answers for themselves. For the menu, only self-set taste tags, such as Not spicy, and the number of people at the table are sent. For the summary, only the week's numbers go: meals eaten together, recipes saved and meals planned, and the weekly average score, steps and sleep of members who agreed and already share them with you, each under a code such as "member A", never a name. Allergies and other foods someone never eats, records and medications, including how many were logged, are never sent; the menu avoids those foods by its own rules. Choose Not now and nothing is sent; the menu plans by its rules and the summary shows plain numbers. Family AI can be turned off at any time.

Who is asked: each member, separately, and a member who chooses Not now still gets the menu and the summary, planned by rules. A managed member, the profile a parent keeps for a child, is the one case where someone answers for someone else. The policy narrows that case twice: the profile is included only if every person who manages it agreed, and then only as its tastes and the number of days something was logged. Our October articles on the Children's Privacy Code and on the tastes a family menu needs meet here.

One subscription, no data

A family plan is a yearly subscription bought by one member that gives Linnea Premium to the members of the family who have their own account. The policy's line on what it shares is four words: it shares no data. Members see whom the plan comes from and when it starts, pauses or ends, never its price, store, renewal date or payment details; the buyer sees how many places are used and which members are covered. The 3.0.1 release notes add that one plan covers up to ten accounts a subscription year besides the subscriber's own, and that when a plan will not renew, the members it covers see the day it ends a week before.

Who is asked: nobody, because the only thing that moves is the subscription. A shared plan is not shared data: buying Premium for a parent shows you nothing of their log that you could not already see, and the policy says so in one sentence.

Invitations without introductions

A new user who signs in with your invitation code gets a month of Premium, and you get one after they log food on three different days within fourteen, up to five a year. We record that they joined with your code and whether they met that condition. We never tell either of you who the other person is or what they log; you see only how many people joined and how many months you earned. The record of who joined with whose code is deleted twelve months after the reward was given or refused.

Who is asked: nobody about the other person, because the two accounts are never introduced. The reward is only for people old enough to use Linnea where they live.

Starting without being asked for an email

You can also use Linnea without signing in: we create an account number for that device only, with no email address, and when you sign in later your entries stay with it. If you do not use Linnea for ninety days, that data is deleted, unless you bought a subscription with it. Sending a family link, joining a family and the family plan need an account; saving a portion from a link does not.

The pattern

The pattern is one we would defend for any family health app. Consent is taken per person, not per household: a family of six is six separate answers. Consent is given for someone else exactly once, for a managed member, and the policy shrinks what it can cover. And the one feature that asks nobody is the one that moves no data. The three questions we offered parents in October still apply: can we read it, can it reach an AI provider, and what happens when the adult who created it leaves. Policy 3.3 answers each in a section short enough to read before you send the first link. None of this is a claim of compliance. It is a description of what the app does and what it asks, the part of a policy that can be held to account.

Linnea 3.0 is on the App Store, and the policy is one page. If a sentence in it leaves you unsure who is asked, tell us which.
